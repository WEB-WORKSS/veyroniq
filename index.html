The problem

Most maintenance is still either too late or too early.

Run-to-failure means a stopped line, emergency parts and overtime. Calendar-based servicing replaces components that still had months of life, and still misses failures that develop between inspections.

Reactive

Fix it when it breaks

  • Unplanned stops mid-shift
  • Expedited parts and call-out labour
  • Secondary damage to connected equipment
  • Scrap and missed orders
Preventive

Service it on a schedule

  • Parts replaced with useful life left
  • Planned work that wasn't needed
  • Failures still slip between intervals
  • Inspection rounds that don't scale
Predictive with Veyroniq

Service it when the data says so

  • Early warning from changing machine behaviour
  • Work planned into scheduled stops
  • Parts ordered ahead of need
  • Technicians sent to the highest-risk asset first
Food processing

Platform

From sensor signal to scheduled work order.

Five capabilities in one workflow: monitor every asset, detect what's abnormal, forecast what it means, recommend the fix and measure the result.

01

Predictive Maintenance & Failure Forecasting

Machine learning models trained on sensor trends and past failures estimate a likely failure window for each monitored component, with a confidence range.

Outcome: maintenance planners can move work into planned shutdowns instead of reacting to breakdowns.

02

Real-Time Machine Health Monitoring

Every connected asset gets a live health score built from its sensor readings and operating context, shown by line, area and site.

Outcome: supervisors see the state of the floor at a glance, without walking it or reading raw tags.

How it works

Four steps from machine to decision.

  1. 01

    Connect equipment

    Sensor data from vibration, temperature and pressure sensors is streamed from the plant, alongside asset lists and maintenance records.

  2. 02

    Learn normal behaviour

    Readings are stored, cleaned and aligned with operating context, and baseline models are built for each asset.

  3. 03

    Detect and forecast

    New data is scored continuously for anomalies, and degrading components get an estimated failure window.

  4. 04

    Recommend and act

    Maintenance teams receive ranked recommendations, schedule the work and close the loop by recording what they found.

FAQ

Common questions

Is Veyroniq available today?

Veyroniq is in initial product development. The demo on this page is an interactive prototype using sample data. Maintenance teams can request early access to take part as the platform is built.

What data does it need?

Readings from vibration, temperature and pressure sensors, a list of assets, and maintenance history such as past work orders and failures. Supported data sources and connection methods will be confirmed with early-access teams.

Do we need new sensors?

Not necessarily. Many plants already collect useful readings. Where coverage is missing, adding sensors to critical assets will be discussed during onboarding.

Does the AI control our equipment?

No. Veyroniq detects, forecasts and recommends. Maintenance teams decide what to do and when, and nothing changes how machines operate.

Where will our data be stored?

The proposed architecture stores data on AWS, with each customer's data isolated and encrypted. Hosting regions will be confirmed with early-access customers.
